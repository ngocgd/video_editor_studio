The mist rolled down from Jade Crane Peak at dawn. Li Wei stood at the edge of the cliff. Below him, the sect's white stairs vanished into the clouds.

Elder Mo had warned him never to climb alone. The spirit beasts grew restless when the moon was full. Li Wei gripped his wooden sword and started up the path.

The alchemy hall smelled of burnt herbs and old bronze. Sister Lan fed another root into the furnace. Its red glow flickered across her tired face.

Nobody in the outer court believed the rumour. A disciple with a broken meridian could not reach Foundation Establishment. Yet every night, a faint blue light glowed in Li Wei's room.

The tournament began with the ringing of the great bell. Hundreds of young cultivators filled the stone arena. Banners of every sect snapped in the cold wind.

His first opponent was a boy from the Iron Fist Clan. He cracked his knuckles and smiled without warmth. Li Wei bowed, as his master had taught him.

The boy struck first, fast as a falling rock. Li Wei stepped aside and let the fist pass his ear. The crowd gasped when the challenger stumbled into the dust.

That evening, Elder Mo called him to the library. Scrolls floated between the towering shelves like sleepy birds. The old man said nothing for a long time.

You have been hiding something, the elder said at last. Show me the light you practise with at night. Li Wei hesitated, then opened his palm.

A small flame rose from his hand, cold and blue. The scrolls around them trembled and turned their pages. Elder Mo's eyes widened for the first time in years.

That flame belonged to the Frost Phoenix, the elder whispered. It vanished from the world three hundred years ago. Whoever carries it will never know peace.

News travels fast in a mountain sect. By morning, three inner disciples were waiting at his door. Their leader wore a jade pendant shaped like a dragon.

Hand over the phoenix technique, the leader said. Or we will take it from your broken body. Li Wei looked at the three of them and sighed.

The fight lasted less than a minute. Frost spread across the courtyard stones like spilled milk. When it melted, the three disciples were kneeling in silence.

Sister Lan found him later by the lotus pond. She handed him a pill that smelled of pine and honey. Eat it, she said, before your meridians freeze for good.

The pill burned all the way down to his dantian. For a moment, he saw the whole mountain glowing with qi. Then the world went dark and quiet.

He woke three days later in the healing pavilion. Rain tapped softly on the paper windows. Someone had left a peach and a folded letter by his bed.

The letter was written in his mother's careful hand. She had died when he was six years old. Yet the ink looked as fresh as morning dew.

Go to the Valley of Ten Thousand Swords, the letter said. Ask the keeper for the name your father gave you. Trust no one who wears the dragon pendant.

Li Wei left the sect before sunrise. He carried only his wooden sword and the peach. The guards at the gate pretended not to see him.

The road wound through bamboo forests and sleeping villages. Farmers bowed to the young traveller in faded grey robes. Children followed him, asking if he could fly.

On the fifth day, bandits blocked the mountain pass. Their leader had a scar from ear to chin. He laughed when he saw the wooden sword.

Li Wei did not draw his blade. He simply breathed out, and the air turned to frost. The bandits fled down the slope, their teeth chattering.

The Valley of Ten Thousand Swords was silent as a tomb. Rusted blades stood in the earth as far as the eye could see. Each one marked the grave of a fallen cultivator.

An old woman sat alone on a stone at the centre of the valley. She was sharpening a needle on a whetstone. She did not look up when he approached.

I know why you have come, the keeper said. Your father's name is written on the oldest sword here. But reading it will cost you a memory.

Li Wei chose to pay the price. He touched the hilt, and a winter storm filled his mind. When it cleared, he could no longer remember his mother's face.

The name on the blade was Li Tianhan, the Frost Sovereign. Every sect in the land had once feared that name. Now it belonged to a boy with a wooden sword.

Far away, in a palace of black jade, a man opened his eyes. He wore a pendant shaped like a coiled dragon. The phoenix has woken, he said, and smiled.

Li Wei turned back toward Jade Crane Peak. The first snow of winter began to fall around him. Somewhere behind the clouds, thunder answered his steps.
